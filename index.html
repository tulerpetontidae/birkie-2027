This is the real profile: the American Birkebeiner Trail's mapped geometry (OpenStreetMap, ODbL), sampled every 100 m against the USGS 3DEP 1-metre bare-earth elevation model — LIDAR, not radar, so tree canopy doesn't inflate it. Covers the mapped trail from the start through Hatchery Creek to the lake's edge (49.15 of 50.00 km, dashed beyond that) — the final lake crossing and Main Street finish aren't part of the permanent trail dataset, but the mapped trail is already flat where it reaches the lakeshore, so the missing stretch is not hiding any climbing.

All four are real measurements, not guesses — they disagree because total climb is sampling-resolution-dependent: finer sampling always counts more small ripples, and unfiltered fine-grained traces (like Trail Genius's) are also prone to counting raw GPS/barometric noise as real elevation change. This page leads with its own measurement — the same real trail geometry sampled every 5 m against USGS 3DEP LIDAR, 20× finer than the 100 m pass used previously — because it's a direct measurement of the actual course rather than a published summary figure. Cumulative pace to the finish is 6.38 km/h. Real terrain says the sting is at the start, not the end: Fire Tower (km 7–12) is the steepest sustained climbing-per-km on the entire course, while it's still early. The back half — Fish Hatchery and End of trail — climbs more gently per kilometre, but it arrives on tired legs and carries the two punchy bumps racers actually talk about, Bitch Hill and Son of Bitch Hill.

* Real data covers this leg only partway (to km 49.15) — the mapped stretch is measured flat, and the final ~0.85 km of lake ice and Main Street pavement isn't in the trail dataset at all.

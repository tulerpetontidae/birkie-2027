Cable to Hayward, 50 km

Real GPS trace, not an estimate: start at ~414 m near Cable, climbing to a high point of 528 m at the Fire Tower (km 11.2), down to a 362 m low crossing toward Lake Hayward. Eight official trail-cabin sections, matched against the actual ski-trail geometry.

This is the real profile: the American Birkebeiner Trail's mapped geometry (OpenStreetMap, ODbL), sampled every 100 m against the USGS 3DEP 1-metre bare-earth elevation model — LIDAR, not radar, so tree canopy doesn't inflate it. Covers the mapped trail from the start through Hatchery Creek to the lake's edge (49.15 of 50.00 km, dashed beyond that) — the final lake crossing and Main Street finish aren't part of the permanent trail dataset, but the mapped trail is already flat where it reaches the lakeshore, so the missing stretch is not hiding any climbing.

All four are real measurements, not guesses — they disagree because total climb is sampling-resolution-dependent: finer sampling always counts more small ripples, and unfiltered fine-grained traces (like Trail Genius's) are also prone to counting raw GPS/barometric noise as real elevation change. Read this page's figure as a floor and Trail Genius's 2,179 m as a ceiling — the true number most likely sits in between, closer to the Worldloppet and independent-GPS figures.

Cumulative pace to the finish is 6.38 km/h. Real terrain says the sting is at the start, not the end: Fire Tower (km 7–12) is the steepest sustained climbing-per-km on the entire course, while it's still early. The back half — Fish Hatchery and End of trail — climbs more gently per kilometre, but it arrives on tired legs and carries the two punchy bumps racers actually talk about, Bitch Hill and Son of Bitch Hill.

* Real data covers this leg only partway (to km 49.15) — the mapped stretch is measured flat, and the final ~0.85 km of lake ice and Main Street pavement isn't in the trail dataset at all.
